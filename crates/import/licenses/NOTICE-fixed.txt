The King James Version the reader shows (1769 standard text)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Source: "King James Version + Apocrypha" (eng-kjv), the standardized text of
1769, courtesy of the CrossWire Bible Society and eBible.org
https://ebible.org/find/details.php?id=eng-kjv
Licence: Public domain (outside the United Kingdom, where the King James
Version is subject to Crown letters patent)

The files under old_testament/ and new_testament/ hold the 66 canonical books,
one verse per line. The italic markers for supplied words and the paragraph
marks were removed, and Psalm superscriptions are stored as verse 0.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Words of Christ (red letter)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
File: data/words_of_jesus.json
Source: the words-of-Jesus markup (\wj) in the same 1769 KJV edition from
CrossWire / eBible.org (https://ebible.org/Scriptures/eng-kjv_usfm.zip)
Licence: Public domain (see above). Extracted as the exact spoken substrings of
each verse and verified to occur character for character in the text.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek texts and lexicons (STEP Bible)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Datasets: TAHOT, TAGNT, TBESH, TBESG
Source: STEP Bible (https://www.STEPBible.org/),
https://github.com/STEPBible/STEPBible-Data
Licence: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0)
https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/
Credit: STEP Bible (www.STEPBible.org). Data created initially by Tyndale
House Cambridge, now curated by STEP Bible.

The files under data/ (TAHOT_*.txt, TAGNT_*.txt, TBESH.txt, TBESG.txt) are
unchanged from STEP Bible's beyond their file names. When loading, the app reads
them as follows: words are keyed to the KJV's verse numbering; the Greek follows
the Textus Receptus behind the KJV (its readings from the variants column replace
differing words, and words absent from it are left out); Hebrew words
reconstructed from the Septuagint and empty Ketiv placeholders are left out; and
a verse is named Aramaic where most of its words are (by the morphology codes).
